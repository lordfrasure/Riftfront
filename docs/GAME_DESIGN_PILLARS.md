# Riftfront game design pillars

| Pillar | Design standard | Evidence to seek |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strategic depth | A consequential planning decision has at least two plausible answers. Their value changes with the forecast, army, or resources, and the choice affects what the player can do later. | Show a situation favoring each answer, explain the mechanism, and observe whether players make and revisit the choice. |
| Scarcity | Scrap, timing, deployment space, or access should bind where a player has competing needs. An investment should displace identifiable defense, coverage, or future flexibility. | Compare the purchases players defer and the resulting outcomes. Banked scrap and occupied pads help diagnose which constraint actually mattered. |
| Opportunity cost | Every investment, branch, or fusion gives up a credible alternative. Economy risks immediate exposure. Fusion trades independent actions and placement for consolidation or specialization. | Compare at a shared decision point with the same starting army and budget. Record both actual spending and capabilities forgone. |
| Counterplay | A threat should test specific army capabilities and have readable cues and more than one affordable answer. A mismatch should have an attributable, recoverable consequence where practical. | Compare equipped and mismatched armies under shared conditions. Ask players to explain the threat and what they would try next. |
| Positioning | Range, spacing, interception, and adjacency should produce competing placements. A linked formation should sometimes benefit from breaking its links to spread or cover another threat. | Move the same units under the same budget and measure where coverage, damage, or routing changes. Verify players can anticipate the consequence. |
| Progression | A timing or specialization choice should change what the player can handle and when. Run and account progression should open approaches without trivializing foundational encounters. | Compare early and delayed commitments and check that a fresh player has a viable path. |
| Player learning | Forecasts should describe the incoming problem. Combat and debrief should expose what happened, including causes of breaches and collapses where the evidence supports them. | Ask players to predict danger before combat and explain the outcome afterward without coaching. Record where the UI fails to convey the needed information. |
| Replayability | Different plans should create different decisions and vulnerabilities. Encounter variation should be forecastable and reproducible while rewarding adaptation. | Compare strategic reversals across curated scenarios and ask what players would intentionally do differently on another run. |

A meaningful feature changes the preferred decision in a specific situation. A support link earns its complexity when adjacency creates a real choice between concentrating damage and spreading against a blast. An additional branch with an identical job and uniformly better output adds recognition and UI burden. Require a proposed feature to name the decision it creates, the viable competing option, the measurable consequence, and how the player learns the rule. If the proposal offers only another purchase or a larger stat block, simplify or defer it.
